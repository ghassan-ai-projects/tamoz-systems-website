      The gap

      Your machines already raise alarms. The cost starts after that.

      Most plants have sensors, PLCs and a SCADA screen. What they lack is something that follows each event until the line is actually healthy, including at 3 a.m.

      How it works

      From machine signal to confirmed recovery

      Tamoz sits above your PLCs, SCADA, MES and maintenance system. It doesn't replace them, and a case stays open until the readings show the problem is gone.

      1. Rules, always on

        Watch

        A deterministic layer turns signals into windowed values and applies your rules around the clock. Normal readings never reach the AI, so they cost nothing.

      2. AI agent, on change only

        Judge

        When the situation changes, the agent wakes. It checks whether the problem is real, what else could explain it and how sure it can be.

      3. AI decides, limits apply

        Act

        It asks your automation for a pre-approved action or alerts the right person. Your own controls decide how far any action goes.

      4. Rules, always on

        Verify

        It keeps watching the readings. "On it" doesn't close a case. If nothing improves, it moves to the next person on the list.

      5. Case memory

        Learn

        It records how each case ended and starts the next one from that history. Proposed improvements are tested on past cases and can be rolled back.

      Every closed case informs the next decision

      Safety limits

      The AI decides what. Your controls decide how much.

      Tamoz is a supervisory layer. It can ask for "start the standby pump", but it never sets a speed or a setpoint, and your plant decides which actions it may request at all.

      Does on its own

      • Groups alarm floods and logs them
      • Alerts people in the order you set and books service jobs
      • Requests small, reversible actions through automation that is safe on its own, such as starting a standby pump

      Asks a person first

      • Stopping a line or anything else you mark as risky
      • Quality decisions, such as scrapping or releasing a batch
      • Approval means tapping a button. A spoken "yes" doesn't count

      Never does

      • Touches E-stops, guarding, interlocks or PLC control loops
      • Takes a risky action on missing or stale data
      • Rewrites its own rules or limits while it runs
      Certified safety stays yours
      Your safety systems stay outside Tamoz and overrule it. It is not a safety component.
      Separate from what it watches
      It runs apart from the systems it monitors, so it notices when a line or a gateway goes quiet.
      A full record
      Who was told what, when, and what the readings did next. Useful at the morning meeting and in the audit.

      Use cases

      The events that fill a night shift

      A new line or utility is added as configuration, without changing the core. Pick any example to watch it play out in the animation.

      Presses and moulding machines

      A tripped cooling pump can stop a line by morning. Tamoz follows oil temperature and coolant flow, alerts people in order and requests the standby pump if you've approved it. It closes the case only when the readings recover.

      Compressed air

      Air use during idle hours points to leaks. Tamoz compares flow with the shift plan, closes an approved valve and books the leak search.

      Machine tools and rotating equipment

      Tamoz connects RMS vibration and temperature trends from your existing monitoring, then books the work for a planned stop.

      Process water and utilities

      Few people cover utilities at night. Tamoz separates real faults from lost links, refuses to act on missing data and calls the on-call technician.

      Alarm floods on the line

      Changeovers and restarts can bury operators in alarms.2 Tamoz checks them against the schedule and turns the flood into one note, or one call if it's real.

      Energy peaks

      A single peak can set the capacity charge.4 Tamoz sees the 15-minute interval heading over target and pauses a load you've marked as flexible, such as forklift charging.

      Process quality

      When an oven or a bath drifts out of spec, Tamoz compares sensors, flags the batch and drafts the deviation report with the evidence attached. Scrap or release stays with your quality team.

      Questions

      What plant teams ask first

      Does Tamoz replace SCADA, MES or our maintenance system?
      No. It sits above them, follows events to an outcome and hands work to the systems you already use.
      How does it connect to our PLCs?
      Through an edge gateway in front of your equipment, which reads the signals you choose. We scope the protocols and network boundaries with your team during pilot preparation.
      Can the AI change setpoints or stop a machine?
      It can't change setpoints. Stopping a line needs a person's approval. Interlocks, E-stops and control loops stay outside Tamoz and overrule it.
      We already have predictive maintenance. Why add this?
      Tamoz uses its output. Condition monitoring tells you a bearing is degrading. Tamoz decides who needs to know, when the work fits and whether the problem was solved.
      Does it analyse raw vibration signals?
      No. It works with values such as RMS and trends. Spectral analysis stays at the edge or in your condition-monitoring system.
      Where does it run, and who owns the data?
      It runs separately from the systems it watches, and we agree hosting with your IT and OT teams. The data stays yours.

      What happens in your plant at 3 a.m.?

      Tell us what you run and who takes the alarm on the night shift. We'll tell you honestly whether a pilot makes sense.
